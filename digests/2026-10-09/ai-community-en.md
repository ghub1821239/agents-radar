Across Dev.to and Lobste.rs, a clear theme emerges: **AI tools are no longer experimental—they’re operational**. Developers are grappling with real-world challenges like cost unpredictability (token bloat, API pricing discrepancies), model hallucination risks, and the brittleness of RAG systems when answers are missing. There’s rising skepticism toward flashy AI demos and a push for transparency—evident in calls for benchmark cards, reproducible testing, and audit trails. Practical patterns are emerging: using local models (Gemma, Flash-Lite) for privacy, optimizing tool output size to cut costs, and treating AI agents as *collaborators*, not replacements. Human-in-the-loop design is increasingly seen as non-negotiable, especially in security-critical contexts. Open-source tooling (LangChain, Burn, agents) is gaining momentum as teams seek control over their AI stack.
